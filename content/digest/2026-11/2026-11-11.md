## D1 — Agentic Engineering

**Frontier coding agents plateau at two-thirds completion on real-world multi-file tasks.** [ExpertCrowd-SWE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13246) introduces 100 expert-authored change requests across 87 repos and 11 languages, with median solutions changing 471 lines. Claude Opus 5.5 leads at 67.3%, GPT-6 Astra at 63.7%, then a steep drop to 46.5% and below. Critically, no task is fully solved by every model across all five runs, and on 80 tasks no model earns full credit every time. Agents take 33–166 median steps per run with little correlation to score. This is the most rigorous evidence yet that current agents operate in a regime of bounded autonomy — they need spec-constrained guardrails, not just bigger models. *(Cross-cuts D4: the variance directly impacts maintenance and rework costs.)*

**LLM agents can be distilled into deterministic, planner-free workflows.** [ReTrace](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14492) extracts the reasoning conditions behind each tool call from logged agent runs, merges them into a compact rule document, and compiles it into an executable workflow where code — not an LLM — selects the next step. On 79 production SOPs, the distilled workflow hits 93.8% completion (vs. 96.8% for the full agent) at 24% lower cost. On τ²-Bench the latency drops from 170s to 1.3s. This is the "specify then compile" pattern applied to agentic process automation. *(Cross-cuts D4: directly attacks inference cost; cross-cuts SCE: rule documents are a form of executable specification.)*

**A sub-THz transmitter designed semi-autonomously by an LLM coding agent.** [TACS-RF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31882) uses Claude Opus 4.8 to drive Cadence Virtuoso, Calibre, EMX, and HFSS to design a 240–251 GHz transmitter in 90nm SiGe BiCMOS. The agent designed VCOs, power drivers, frequency doublers, antennas, and matching networks, with the resulting chain delivering +5.1 to +6.3 dBm across an 11 GHz tuning range. The described human/agent division of labor is instructive: the human sets specs and makes process-technology decisions; the agent iterates through simulation-driven design loops. This is D1 pushed into a domain (analog RF ASIC design) where the "10× with human on the loop" thesis faces its hardest test.

**Forethought: neurosymbolic primitives make agent reasoning verifiable and cheaper.** [Forethought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04096) (v2) builds agentic workflows from symbolic and neural primitives composed through a typed DSL, where every step carries an output contract checked at design time and enforced at execution time. The system improves end-to-end tool-calling accuracy by a median 32% over direct prompting, and small open models paired with Forethought match frontier-model performance. Removing the contract-and-control layer erases most gains, proving it's the checked structure, not decomposition alone, that matters. *(Cross-cuts D4 and SCE: contracts as datasheets for agent steps.)*

**ActiveSecHarness: compositional security as a structural property of agentic coding.** [This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13960) argues that security in AI coding harnesses is a structural property of the cognitive architecture, not an emergent property of models. The system uses Macaroon-inspired capability tokens across four trust rings, a 2D taint lattice, and deterministic Ring-0 guards that achieve perfect attack-block rate and zero false positives in 12.7μs — 11,200× faster than an LLM judge. The framing of "Separation of Intelligence and Authority" is directly applicable to any agentic engineering practice deploying agents with shell/filesystem/network access. *(Cross-cuts D3 and D4: reduces incident surface and support cost.)*

## D3 — Build for Agents

**Impossibility results bound what gatekeepers can certify for agent-produced code.** [This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13367) proves that no finite rulebook can both certify every safe program and reject every unsafe one when the rules involve "never" (safety) and "eventually" (liveness) properties. Violations can always be caught by watching, but no safe program can be cleared for good. The practical implication for agent interoperability: any B2A or A2A contract that requires runtime safety guarantees must be structured as checkable whitelists (everything it must allow, nothing it must refuse) rather than complete rulesets. Time-bounded execution makes everything checkable but at the cost of running the program for essentially the whole limit. This is a fundamental constraint on agent trust boundaries.

**Veritas auto-generates verifiers for agent tasks, improving self-improvement loops.** [Veritas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13336) addresses the bottleneck of hand-written verifiers that judge agent outputs. It generates verifiers automatically using an evidence header (what the verifier will observe), a domain-rule library, and self-improving authoring principles. Generated verifiers beat seven other harnesses on all four execution-scored benchmarks. For D3, this matters because agent interoperability requires programmatic verification of agent outputs — Veritas shows this can be automated rather than hand-crafted per task. *(Cross-cuts D1 and SCE: verification generation is the agent analogue of automated test generation from specs.)*

## D4 — Cost of Ownership

**Prefix cache metastability creates a silent, persistent throughput collapse that breaks the 2×/½× equation by doubling effective inference cost after a transient burst.** [This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13412) demonstrates that LLM agent serving with prefix caching is metastable: a few minutes of excess arrivals can leave a vLLM server at ~10% cache hit ratio and ~50% throughput for the remaining 70 minutes of a run, while an identical non-burst twin stays healthy. The failure appears on both vLLM and SGLang, with both Qwen3-32B and SWE-bench-shaped workloads. Throttling new admissions recovered only 1 of 7 collapsed runs; suspending active sessions against a capacity-based KV budget recovered all of them. The prescribed fix — a budgeter that bounds resident KV demand to pool size — needs no per-workload tuning. This is a production-critical finding for any team running agentic workloads at scale.

**Agent trajectory failures are overwhelmingly detectable in-flight but go unacted upon, meaning verification checkpoints directly reduce downstream incident and support costs.** [The trajectory failure study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13254) analyzed 95 failed multi-agent runs and found faults enter at median 18% of the run. In 72% of failures, evidence against the error was already in the agent's context; in 88% of those, no agent acted on it. Task contracts fixed at admission, environment re-checks, and independent judges reviewing unused evidence cover most failure episodes. Additionally, 45% of Who&When benchmark failures were actually content-filter crashes misattributed as agent failures — a failure class invisible to standard taxonomies. This directly informs where to place human-on-the-loop checkpoints for maximum incident reduction.

**ReTrace's distillation approach cuts per-task inference cost by 24% while reducing latency by 130×.** As noted in D1, [ReTrace](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14492) converts LLM-planned workflows into code-driven ones. The cost structure shifts from per-invocation LLM calls to a one-time distillation cost amortized over all subsequent runs. For SOPs executed thousands of times, this transforms the token-cost curve from linear to near-flat.

## Software Civil Engineering Lens

Today's batch is the strongest single-day signal for the SCE thesis in recent memory. Five of twelve papers explicitly argue that **checked structure** — not model capability — is the primary driver of reliable agent output.

**Formal specification as the control plane is gaining empirical support.** Forethought's result — that [removing the contract-and-control layer erases most accuracy gains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04096) — is direct evidence that the "blueprints" pillar of SCE (formal spec) isn't aspirational but immediately practical. ReTrace's rule documents are an emergent form of executable specification extracted from agent behavior, echoing the Event Modeling idea that specifications should be the primary artifact. ProofCoder's finding that [continuous reasoning with feedback-guided revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14106) outperforms rigid tactic loops maps directly to the Specify → Plan → Verify → Apply → Observe lifecycle.

**The impossibility theorem sharpens the "bounded autonomy" concept.** The [proof that no finite rulebook can certify every safe program](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13367) is a theoretical foundation for why bounded autonomy must be the operating model. You cannot build a complete safety gate; you can only build checkable whitelists within defined boundaries. This is the mathematical justification for the Decider pattern: agents operate within spec-constrained envelopes, and the spec is what makes the envelope auditable.

**The trajectory failure study is the closest thing to a "material datasheet" for agent reliability.** [Knowing that faults enter at the 18th percentile of a run](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13254), that 72% of failures had ignored counter-evidence, and that environment re-checks cover most episodes — these are the kind of empirical properties that civil engineers would record on a datasheet for a structural material. The field needs more of this: quantified failure modes with prescribed controls, not anecdotal post-mortems.

**Hardware verification is converging on the same pattern.** Both [TACS-RF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31882) (agent-driven analog design verified through EM simulation) and [Agentic AI Meets Formal Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13705) (proof-native hardware development) show the Specify → Simulate → Verify loop being adopted in hardware, where the consequences of unverified designs are physical and expensive. The parallel between "proof-native hardware development" and "spec-driven software development" is exact.

The gap analysis: **licensure and education** remain the least-addressed pillars. No paper today proposes who should be accountable when an agent-produced artifact fails in production, or how engineers should be trained to specify rather than implement. The technical infrastructure for professionalization is advancing faster than the institutional infrastructure.
