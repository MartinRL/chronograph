## D1 — Agentic Engineering

**Runtime contracts are the enforcement mechanism prompts can't be.** A controlled study across 460 runs on two model families shows that [executable contracts enforced during agent execution produced zero oracle-detected violations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19549) in 104 paired runs, versus 21–55% violation rates without rules. Crucially, prompted rules stopped some violation types but failed to prevent destructive side effects like recursive directory deletes — operations that post-generation checks can't undo because the command already ran. This is the strongest empirical evidence yet that agentic engineering requires the **Specify → Verify → Apply** lifecycle, not just better prompts. *(Cross-cuts D4: downstream incident reduction.)*

**CompoundingKernel demonstrates self-improving agent search at scale.** A system of [over 1,000 parallel agents writing GPU kernels from zero achieved #1 on SOL-ExecBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19230), producing kernels 4.4× faster than reference baselines across 235 problems. The architecture — an LLM controller selecting parents from a lineage archive, correctness checks outside the model, and parallel child agents — is a clean instantiation of the agentic engineering loop. The key scaling insight: **the rate of reliable measurements, not agent count, is the bottleneck.** One warm-restart fix alone raised evaluation throughput 3.6×. This maps directly to agentic pipeline design: invest in your verification infrastructure, not just parallelism.

**On-policy distillation makes agentic coding capabilities transferable at lower cost.** [SWE-OPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21020) shows that teacher supervision from a 27B model lifts a 9B student's SWE-bench Pro resolve rate from 35.9% to 39.4%. The offline variant with cached teacher scores cuts GPU-hours by 59–73% and halves peak GPU allocation. Cross-tokenizer distillation (DeepSeek → GLM) yielded ~4% improvement. This is directly actionable for teams running agentic coding pipelines: you can train smaller, cheaper models that retain meaningful agent capability. *(Cross-cuts D4: inference cost reduction.)*

**Formal threshold theory for agent plan commitment replaces ad-hoc retry logic.** [A new paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19663) derives optimal policies for when agents should repair, replan, or abandon a broken plan. The core result: the optimal policy is a threshold in *effective disruption depth* — shallow damage warrants repair, deep damage warrants replanning, catastrophic damage warrants abandonment. Under repeated disruptions, **patch debt accumulates** until it outweighs repair's cost advantage, at which point the agent should rebuild. As LLM replanning costs approach zero, the "stability price" (cost others pay when you change your plan) becomes the sole force sustaining plan commitment. This gives agentic orchestration designers a principled replacement for ad-hoc retry loops.

**Turing Agent achieves 57-62% faithful formalization on research-level math.** [The system](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21299) orchestrates end-to-end formal verification through a two-scale architecture of theorem and proof formalization agents with structured decomposition and parallel verification. It completed formal verification of three previously unresolved TCS tasks, with one requiring ~100 formally verified auxiliary claims. While the domain is mathematics, the architecture pattern — decompose, parallelize, verify, accumulate experience — is the blueprint for any agentic verification pipeline.

## D2 — AI in the Product

**Deterministic validation gates make LLM-driven engineering analysis trustworthy.** In a [satellite vibration testing application](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20579), LLMs extracted 76-field configurations from Chinese test records with F1 of 0.98–1.0, but the real contribution is the gate paradigm: typed configurations are consumed only after passing deterministic checks on units, physical consistency, verbatim quotes, cross-model agreement, and measurement consistency. With all gates active, 20/20 configurations produced bit-identical results to gold runs, and all 17 real and injected errors were caught (16 would have passed without gates). This is a template for embedding LLMs in safety-critical product workflows. *(Cross-cuts SCE: deterministic gates as "codes and norms" for AI-generated outputs.)*

**Specification explicitness is the control surface for LLM-generated code quality.** A [72-participant controlled experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20195) found that lightweight structural specs reduced failures by only 8.7%, while explicit behavioral obligations (invariants, edge cases, exception handling) cut failures by 47.2% and distinct failure modes by 20.5%. Developers without explicit obligations overestimated generated-code correctness by ~23 percentage points. For product teams using AI code generation: the investment in specification quality has a measurable, steep return curve — but only beyond a threshold of behavioral explicitness.

## D3 — Build for Agents

**A2A Agent Cards present a real, measurable pre-invocation attack surface.** Researchers [probed 36.1 million domains and found 310 deployed A2A Agent Cards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18887), then characterized 10 categories of discovery-time manipulation attacks. The SECCARD validation framework achieves 97% security recall at 1.7% FPR with the best semantic judge, but recall across nine LLM judges varies from 25.8% to 92.3%. This is the first empirical measurement of the A2A ecosystem's real-world footprint and its security posture. For anyone building B2A interfaces or agent marketplaces: **discovery-time trust is not a theoretical problem; it's a 310-node live surface with demonstrated attack vectors.**

**Authorization completeness in agent workflows has measurable gaps.** [Auditable Authorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19237) formalizes refusal safety and bounded authorization completeness across model, harness, and workflow layers. The findings are sobering: parallel confirmation loses approved branches in 3 tasks per model, CrewAI restoration omits 50% of approvals, and 4 of 59 n8n workflows execute after Reject. The paper proposes an audit contract making both refusal safety and authorization completeness independently verifiable. For anyone building agent-to-agent systems: human-in-the-loop approval is currently a leaky abstraction at every layer. *(Cross-cuts D4: incident risk from authorization failures.)*

## D4 — Cost of Ownership

**Conventional token accounting underestimates agentic inference costs by 25×, threatening the 2× output / ½× cost equation.** Analysis of [nearly one billion tokens across 42 real-world SE agent sessions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20873) reveals that cached-context reads constitute 94.8% of all API-reported tokens — for every generated token, ~260 cached tokens are read. Context grows 5.3× over a session. A cache-aware energy estimator puts the workload at ~250 kWh versus ~10 kWh from output-based estimators like EcoLogits. Subagent delegation compounds the problem: parent logs undercount delegated tokens by 10×. Starting each task from fresh context would cut the estimate by ~60%, suggesting that **context management hygiene is the highest-leverage cost lever** for agentic engineering teams. If your cost model ignores cached-context reads, your ½× target is fiction.

**Datadog's production experimentation for AI agents closes the observability gap in agentic operations.** [Datadog is extending its Agent Observability platform with Online Experiments](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/datadog-ai-agent-observability/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering), enabling teams to compare agent versions against real production traffic rather than synthetic evals. This directly addresses a D4 pain point: without production-grade observability, teams can't distinguish whether agentic speed gains are creating downstream incident and support burden. The shift from predefined eval datasets to live traffic comparison is analogous to the canary/blue-green deployment discipline that mature SRE practices require — now applied to agent behavior.

**Distillation-based model compression delivers 60-73% GPU-hour savings.** The [SWE-OPD offline variant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21020) with cached teacher scores halves peak GPU allocation while maintaining benchmark-competitive accuracy. For teams operating agentic coding pipelines at scale, this is a direct reduction in the cost of the agentic factory — smaller models with transferred capability mean lower per-inference cost and simpler infrastructure. The cross-tokenizer result (DeepSeek → GLM) further reduces vendor lock-in risk.

## Software Civil Engineering Lens

Today's batch is remarkably SCE-dense — five of twelve items scored 5 on the SCE dimension, and they collectively advance multiple pillars of the professionalization thesis.

**The specification thesis gets its strongest empirical validation yet.** The [specification explicitness study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20195) provides hard numbers for what SCE has long argued: that specification is the critical control surface. The 47% failure reduction from explicit behavioral obligations vs. 9% from lightweight structure is not a gradual improvement curve — it's a phase transition. Lightweight structure (the equivalent of informal sketches) barely registers; explicit behavioral obligations (the equivalent of engineering blueprints) transform outcomes. This is the "formal spec" pillar in action, with controlled experimental evidence. The developer calibration finding (23pp overconfidence without specs) further validates the "human on the loop" model: without formal specs, humans can't even accurately judge the output.

**Runtime contracts are the "building codes" of agentic software.** The [Beyond Prompts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19549) paper demonstrates that executable contracts enforced during agent execution function exactly like building codes — they constrain the construction process, not just the design. The finding that post-generation checks can't repair already-executed destructive commands mirrors the physical engineering reality that you can't un-pour concrete. This is the **Specify → Plan → Verify → Apply → Observe** lifecycle made concrete: verification must happen *before* application, not after.

**Deterministic validation gates are "material datasheets" for LLM outputs.** The [satellite vibration testing gates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20579) instantiate the concept of material datasheets — standardized, deterministic tests that any LLM output must pass before it enters a critical calculation. The 20/20 bit-identical result with gates active, versus 16/17 errors passing without them, is the kind of reliability delta that separates engineering from craft.

**The authorization audit paper exposes the gap between procedural and enforceable safety.** The [auditable authorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19237) finding that CrewAI omits 50% of approvals and n8n workflows execute after Reject is a stark illustration of why "human in the loop" as currently implemented is craft-level safety theater, not engineering-grade control. The proposed audit contract — making refusal safety and authorization completeness independently verifiable — is precisely the kind of formal guarantee that SCE's "codes and norms" pillar demands.

**The plan commitment theory provides the analytical foundation for bounded autonomy.** The [threshold theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19663) formalizes when agents should operate within their current plan (bounded autonomy) versus when they should escalate to a higher control plane (replanning or abandonment). The concept of "patch debt" — accumulated repair cost that eventually forces a rebuild — directly maps to tech debt dynamics in software systems, but now with a closed-form optimality condition. This is simulation for agent behavior, the "terraform plan" analog the SCE thesis calls for.

Taken together, today's findings suggest the professionalization of software is accelerating on the specification, codes/norms, and simulation pillars simultaneously. The gap remains widest on licensure and education.
