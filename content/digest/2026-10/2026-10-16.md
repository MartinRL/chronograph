## D1 — Agentic Engineering

**Four Gemini 4 Argon agents autonomously beat Minecraft's Ender Dragon in 27 hours**, writing 2,783 programs, self-assigning roles, sharing learned strategies via in-game chat and shared memory, and recovering from 605 deaths — all without human intervention or pre-written game logic. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319) frames this as a proxy for long-horizon software engineering: maintaining a coherent plan across thousands of dependent steps for over a day. The practical D1 takeaway isn't the game — it's that the coordination substrate (shared memory, natural-language protocol, self-organized division of labor) held together at scale without any explicit orchestration framework. The failure modes (605 deaths = ~22 per hour) show the cost of relying on retry-based resilience rather than planning guarantees.

**Athanor demonstrates autonomous post-training by a team of four Claude Opus 5.5 agents**, each on an 8-GPU node, that improved Qwen3-4B-Base from 15.9 to 67.3 on a dev suite (matching the official instruct model) in a 104-hour campaign — [with no human research advice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21055). The agents recombined known methods (SFT, RL, weight merges, self-distillation) autonomously. This is D1 at its most recursive: agents building better models. The Athanor harness itself is the interesting architecture — frozen evaluation, decontaminated data, persistent research records — essentially a sandbox that lets agents research freely while the harness owns integrity. (Also relevant to D4: the 104 GPU-hours × 4 nodes cost is non-trivial; ROI only works if the resulting model is production-useful.)

**NVIDIA's open midtraining recipe for agentic post-training** [shows that a 256K-context midtraining stage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22019) on natural software-engineering data (packed repos, PRs, commit histories, build logs) plus a small share of agentic traces significantly improves downstream agentic performance: SWE-bench Verified jumps from 60.7→64.5 on Nemotron 3.5 Lightning with 500B midtraining tokens. Crucially, they found that base-model proxy metrics did not correlate reliably with post-training results — you have to evaluate the full pipeline. For teams fine-tuning their own models for agentic use, this is an actionable recipe with published ablations.

**RAC (Relational Agent Contract) separates semantic decisions from mechanical derivation** in constrained engineering tasks. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20935) shows that when agents decide *which relations* an artifact should satisfy but delegate geometric/timing derivation to specialized tools, clean-pass rates hit 90.9% vs. 74.0% for requirements-only delegation across PCB layout, job-shop scheduling, and embedded firmware tasks. This is a clean instantiation of the "agents propose, tools verify" pattern. (Cross-cutting D3: the RAC contract is harness-agnostic and could serve as an interoperability layer.)

**Independent data-integrity verification for LLM-driven hardware coverage closure** [demonstrates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21570) a design-agnostic agent that pushed functional coverage from 67–92% to 100% on three RTL designs within 8 iterations. The key insight: coverage is read from the simulation's own exported YAML, never from the model's self-report, and data integrity is checked by independent scoreboards. A scoreboard monitor even caught a clock-domain-crossing bug in the *human-written* stimulus code. The paper is refreshingly honest about its limitations (three designs, no baseline, no ablation), but the architecture — ground-truth feedback loops separated from agent output — is the transferable pattern.

## D3 — Build for Agents

**The Agent Status Protocol (ASP) v0.1 proposes a lightweight observability standard for multi-agent systems**, using [JSON events over HTTP POST and SSE for state streaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21538). The critical design decision: ASP does not trust agent self-reports. The server infers liveness from missed heartbeats, detects alive-but-stuck agents, and accepts observations from supervisors, guardrails, operators, and peers — with precedence rules over agent self-assessment. State is modeled along five independent dimensions (lifecycle, health, task, floor, governance). It deliberately reuses A2A task states, Open Floor Protocol floor states, and OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions. This is exactly the kind of interoperability primitive the D3 layer needs — not another agent-to-agent protocol, but an observability contract that lets heterogeneous agents be monitored uniformly.

**The Global Coherence paper provides formal impossibility bounds for multi-agent coordination**: [the Observation-Aliasing Impossibility Theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02036) proves that when k indistinguishable world states require pairwise-disjoint actions, no amount of reasoning, messages, or retries can beat 1/k success probability. The practical upshot: the *harness must own shared state and govern commit*. On tau2-bench Telecom, current-state checks score 0.07 after silent reverts; harness-governed commit scores 1.00. For anyone building A2A or MCP-based multi-agent systems, this paper mathematically confirms that state ownership cannot be left to individual agents.

**FAOP's normal forms for agent workflows** [also serve D3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22061): the FNF-4 (Scoped) normal form confines every workflow step to least-privilege tool access, and the typed contract on each atomic step creates a natural interoperability boundary. If your agent workflows are consumed by other agents or external systems, having each step's contract be type-checked and scope-confined is an interop prerequisite.

## D4 — Cost of Ownership

**Code quality directly predicts the token cost of agentic code review.** A [pre-registered study of 465 agent-authored PRs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21914) across 246 repositories found that each CodeHealth point is associated with 3.5–6.1% lower token cost in a five-agent review-and-fix pipeline, with 95% confidence intervals excluding zero. The association was located in the review stage, where agents iterated more on unhealthy code without producing more comments — they churned. This directly validates the 2× output / ½× downstream cost equation: if your agentic factory produces low-quality code faster, the review agents burn more tokens fixing it, partially erasing the speed gain. The refactoring ROI argument now has quantitative support in agentic-review economics.

**JevAgentHarness cuts agent inference cost by 81–89% on in-distribution tasks** by [delegating routine tool-choice decisions to a 4B decision model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21412) and calling the frontier LLM only when the small model's confidence falls below a fitted threshold. On out-of-distribution tasks, the agent still solved 91.5% with AgentDecision-4B, losing at most 3 points of task success. The key engineering insight: the decision model must be trained on states the agent reaches *after its own mistakes*, not just clean trajectories. This is the most directly actionable cost-reduction technique in today's batch — applicable to any agentic loop where most steps are routing decisions.

**Meta's Diviner agentic RCA system** [operates at production scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21281) with a four-stage reliability lifecycle (Encode → Execute → Evaluate → Adapt) that directly addresses incident response costs. The Encode stage converts reviewed incidents and service-owner expertise into versioned, scoped skills. The Adapt stage uses on-call feedback to derive knowledge updates, admitted only after replay, quality checks, and owner approval — human on the loop, not in the loop. By measuring "exact cause," "useful direction," and "Groundedness" against responder-reviewed outcomes, Diviner creates a closed loop between incident cost and agent improvement. This is agentic SRE at scale, targeting the most expensive downstream cost center (2nd/3rd-line support and MTTR).

## Software Civil Engineering Lens

Today's batch is unusually rich for SCE. Three papers independently converge on the same thesis from different angles:

**FAOP is the most explicit SCE artifact to date.** [Its four progressive normal forms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22061) (Decomposed → Typed → Factored → Scoped) are directly analogous to building codes: they prescribe structural requirements that every workflow must satisfy before deployment. The algebra of provably sound equalities for refactoring is the "structural calculation" of agent workflows. The +V (verified termination), +O (optimization), and +B (time/cost bounds) extensions are literally the "material datasheets" — declaring what a workflow step guarantees. Coming from JPMorgan, this is a regulated-industry player demanding engineering discipline, not an academic exercise.

**Phase-separated completeness auditing in Verus** [demonstrates a fundamental tension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20676) in specification-driven development: correctness feedback suppresses the evidence needed for completeness auditing. The solution — separating the discovery phase (find witnesses that satisfy the contract but violate intent) from the repair phase (fix the spec) — is a direct analogue of separating structural testing from structural design in civil engineering. You can't fix what you've already hidden. The 81.3% vs. 62.5% implementation success rate for phase-revised vs. corrective-feedback-revised contracts quantifies the cost of conflating these phases.

**The Global Coherence impossibility theorem** [provides the first formal "load-bearing capacity" calculation for multi-agent systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02036): given k indistinguishable states requiring disjoint actions, the system *provably cannot* exceed 1/k success. This is the equivalent of proving a beam will fail under a given load — no amount of better materials (smarter models) can overcome insufficient information (insufficient state visibility). The practical engineering response — harness-governed commit, counterexample-guided context refinement — is exactly the kind of simulation + verification workflow SCE predicts.

Together, these papers suggest the professionalization is accelerating. We're seeing formal specifications (FAOP), impossibility theorems establishing design limits (Global Coherence), independent verification separated from agent self-assessment (Verus, hardware coverage, ASP), and lifecycle-governed knowledge management (Diviner). The missing pillars remain licensure and standardized education, but the technical foundations are solidifying faster than expected.
