## D1 — Agentic Engineering

**Code understanding, not code editing, is the true bottleneck for coding agents.** CABRA, a new synthetic benchmark built on call-graph transformations, demonstrates that while agents stay near-perfect on editing tasks by offloading to tools like grep, they fail when tasks require understanding divergent logic across classes. Critically, on SWE-bench Verified, [tool call counts for reading and analysis predict agent accuracy better than lines of code edited](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10610). This reframes how we should think about agent difficulty: the cognitive work is in comprehension, not mechanical patching. For agentic engineering practices, this suggests investing in better code retrieval and summarization tooling rather than edit-generation capabilities.

**A principled framework for deciding when to evolve the harness vs. train model weights.** Tian et al. introduce a "diagnose-then-intervene" methodology: [label failed trajectories by the first failure signal — process failures (blocked calls, loops, budget exhaustion) are harness problems; content failures (poor plans) are weight problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11655). On DeepPlanning, self-evolving harness loops lifted Qwen3.5-4B from 0.16 to 0.30 and delivery from 55% to 90%. LoRA adapters trained on evolved-harness trajectories internalized the behavioral gains, with the adapter alone matching the full evolution line on 9B. This is directly actionable: classify your agent failures before choosing your optimization strategy. Also relevant to **D4** — the framework reduces wasted training compute.

**Closed-loop evaluation of LLM coding agents on embedded firmware tasks.** García-Carrasco et al. present a [benchmark where agents must translate plain-text engineering descriptions into ESP32 firmware, self-verify, and iterate through build-and-runtime feedback loops](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11447). Across 420 runs, gpt-5.4 leads but doesn't saturate; qwen3.5-27B is the strongest local model; smaller models degrade sharply. The key insight for D1 practitioners: embedded/hardware-adjacent domains demand closed-loop correctness under timing and safety constraints that pure code-generation benchmarks miss entirely. This is where the "verify → apply → observe" cycle from SCE becomes non-negotiable.

**NOMOS compiles natural-language policies into deterministic, statically verified tool-call gates.** This [four-pass compiler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11030) transforms written policies into microsecond-latency enforcement gates without requiring a prover, solver, or LLM at runtime. On τ²-bench, violations among state-changing calls drop from 66.3% to 2.6% (airline) and 30.8% to 6.9% (retail). The static verification pass catches and repairs 37% of candidate rules that would otherwise be inoperable — a reminder that naive rule extraction is insufficient. Achieves zero attack success rate on banking tasks. Also highly relevant to **D3** (agents consuming agent-facing APIs need policy gates) and **D4** (microsecond enforcement vs. per-step LLM verifier calls).

## D2 — AI in the Product

**Generative UIs reduce agent interaction from 3.4 dialogue rounds to 1.2.** GenUI-Harness pairs a [Tool Agent with a GUI Coder Agent that generates ephemeral structured front-end interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11123) instead of forcing users through text-based dialogue. The system addresses the RL training challenge for UI generation through Dynamic UX (sandbox-based reward collection) and Reward Auditor (meta-reward mechanism to prevent reward hacking). A trained 4B backbone reaches 58% Pass@3, outperforming Claude Opus 5 at 46.67%. The strategic implication: conversational interfaces are a local maximum — the next generation of agent UIs will be dynamically generated, task-specific structured interfaces. This significantly reduces the cognitive load on users, which is itself a **D4** downstream cost (fewer support escalations from confused users).

## D3 — Build for Agents

**Study contracts formalize the boundary between what output-only review can and cannot verify for research agents.** Waks and Glocker propose [binding declared experimental choices, run obligations, and claim scope to recorded execution evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11754), distinguishing contract-relative verification from truth. A deterministic checker detected all eight registered mutations, while LLM judges without contract context missed cases. The pattern is relevant beyond research: any agent-to-agent handoff where the consuming agent needs to verify the producing agent's work faces the same information asymmetry. Contract-based verification is a prerequisite for trustworthy A2A workflows.

**Governance contracts can safely admit model-produced observations when bounded by admission policies.** The [sufficiency-checked governance contract pattern](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10978) treats model outputs as probabilistic sensor readings with declared false-positive ceilings, while keeping decision authority deterministic. The probability that sensing changes a verdict is bounded by the union of admitted-wrong and unknown rates across sensed fields. This is a design pattern for any B2A or agent-mediated decision system operating in regulated domains — the model senses, the contract decides.

## D4 — Cost of Ownership

**OnTrack saves ~18% of compute burned on failing agent runs through millisecond-per-step streaming monitoring.** This directly improves the 2× output / ½× downstream cost equation by eliminating wasted inference tokens on runs headed for failure. [OnTrack compares live agent steps against recorded successful trajectories using structure-aware optimal transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12375), alerting or blocking within ~1ms per step. On SWE-bench trajectories, 83% of aborted runs were actually heading to failure (5 of 6 aborts correct). The approach works across three access regimes — full historical reference, schema-only, and zero prior knowledge — making it deployable even without extensive trajectory databases. Unlike safeguard agents that add cost per step or post-hoc evaluation that burns tokens before flagging issues, this sits at the sweet spot of real-time, near-zero-overhead observability.

**Cross-model KV cache reuse via selective recomputation cuts inference cost dramatically for multi-model agent systems.** [RaReCache enables a small model to prefill KV caches for a much larger target model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11358), recomputing only the 30% of positions where transfer fails (identified by rank disagreement). Results: 3.04× prefill speedup, 1.8× request throughput on a single GPU, and 5.0×/6.4× reduction in median/p99 time-to-first-token. For agentic systems that route between models — e.g., a small model for easy queries escalating to a large model for hard ones — this eliminates the cold-start penalty that made cascading architectures expensive.

**Compliance-bounded self-evolution confines agent adaptation to the runtime harness, enabling auditability.** The [admission gate pattern](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10629) for credit pipelines admitted only 144 of 7,449 candidate changes, none of which worsened error on held-out history. Without the gate, 309 harmful changes were admitted, leaving missed flags above 10% in 49 of 90 runs. This is a maintenance and regulatory cost reducer: every adaptation is a diff with a cause and test attached, mapped to EU AI Act provisions for high-risk scoring. The cost of not having such gates is measured in regulatory incidents and model-risk reviews.

## Software Civil Engineering Lens

Today's batch is unusually rich for the SCE thesis, with multiple papers advancing different pillars simultaneously.

**Formal specification is getting its evaluation framework.** The [holistic SpecGen evaluation paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10604) directly addresses the gap between "proof checks out" and "specification captures intent." Their finding that a specification can achieve 100% postcondition recall while accepting 0% of required inputs is a perfect illustration of why the SCE thesis demands formal spec as a *pillar*, not a checkbox. A checked proof against a wrong spec is the software equivalent of a structurally sound bridge to the wrong riverbank. The framework's separation of input acceptance from output correctness maps cleanly to the Decider pattern's precondition/postcondition distinction.

**Bounded autonomy is being formalized from multiple angles.** Three papers today converge on the same SCE principle — agents operating reliably within spec constraints — from different domains. [NOMOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11030) compiles natural-language policies into deterministic gates (codes/norms pillar). The [harness-as-mutable-surface paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10629) confines self-evolution to reviewable diffs (licensure/audit pillar). And [Lu's verification-theoretic framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10611) proves that uniformly bounded self-modification under a fixed verification protocol remains within the same verification class — establishing a *mathematical ceiling* on what self-improvement can achieve without changing the verification regime. This is the "bounded autonomy" concept getting its theoretical foundation.

**The simulate-before-deploy pattern is maturing.** OnTrack's trajectory monitoring against reference runs, the admission gate's simulation before deployment, and the embedded firmware benchmark's closed-loop verification all implement variants of the "simulate → verify → apply" lifecycle. The critical shift: simulation is moving from a development-time activity to a *runtime* activity, with agents continuously being checked against expected trajectories. This is the "terraform plan for domain logic" idea applied to agent behavior.

**The harness-vs-weights paper is arguably the most SCE-relevant finding today.** The [failure composition taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11655) — process failures belong to the harness (the engineered scaffold), content failures belong to the model (the material) — is a direct analogy to civil engineering's separation of design (how you build with the material) from material science (what the material can do). You don't blame the steel when the truss design is wrong, and you don't redesign the truss when the steel is weak. This kind of principled separation is exactly what SCE predicts will emerge as the field professionalizes.
