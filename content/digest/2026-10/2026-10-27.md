## D1 — Agentic Engineering

**AGENTS.md is becoming the de facto spec artifact for coding agents, but it's half-baked.** A [census of 22,854 popular GitHub repositories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31715) finds agent instruction files (AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md) in 26.3% of repos overall and 59.6% of the most-starred. Across 341,852 annotated statements, the content is a practical mix of project knowledge, build rules, and workflow constraints — but 74.0% of directives give no reason for the rule. Projects also disagree fundamentally on AI attribution: commit-message rules more often prohibit it than require it, while PR rules more often require disclosure. A third of instruction sets show generator signatures or AI-marked creation commits, and four in five are subsequently hand-edited. This is the embryo of a specification language for agentic collaboration, but without rationale, agents can't generalize and humans can't audit intent. *Also relevant to D3 and SCE.*

**EvoSkill v2 demonstrates stateful, self-improving agent instruction editing.** [This three-agent system](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25931) — benchmark adaptor, solver, and stateful evolver — rewrites the instruction "skills" given to an agent across epochs while keeping the model and harness frozen. The key architectural choice: the evolver is a single stateful coding agent maintaining context across the entire run, outperforming both single-call optimizers (SkillOpt) and per-round agents (SkillBoost) by 5–8 percentage points on held-out benchmarks. Critically, the evolver's cost-per-percentage-point-gained is lower than either baseline. A demonstration with Claude Haiku 5.5 powering both solver and evolver shows a 21.5pp self-improvement gain, establishing that even modest models can profitably refine their own operating instructions. *Also relevant to D4 — this is a concrete pattern for reducing inference cost while improving output quality.*

**Warped tool output formatters are a silent reliability killer in agent systems.** [Research from a production RAG system](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32110) at the Pilsudski Institute reveals that tools can compute correctly yet mislead agents through their output formatting — omitting search limits, result caps, or identifier namespaces. In controlled tests, fixing formatter wording alone flipped verifier acceptance from 90/90 incorrect acceptances to 90/90 correct rejections. The researchers provide an audit prompt (tested with Codex and Claude Code) that, when pointed at their own system, rediscovered known defects plus twelve new ones. Applied to OpenClaw and Hermes — two prominent open-source agent frameworks — it found warped formatters in both, with the OpenClaw fix already merged. This is a high-leverage, low-effort intervention for any team running tool-using agents. *Cross-cuts D3 (tool interface contracts) and D4 (incident prevention).*

**Authorization and recovery in durable agent workflows need formal decomposition.** [A conformance study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31916) isolates four distinct obligations in agent recovery: checkpoint state, dispatch permission, evidence of external effect, and reconciliation permission. Testing 336 histories across LangGraph and Temporal hosts with 14 schedules and three provider contracts, retained-ledger reconciliation passes all 42 histories while simpler retry strategies (cached, revalidation, stable-key) pass only 30–36. The paper frames recovery correctness as independent of LLM behavior — this is pure systems engineering applied to agent infrastructure. *Also relevant to D4 (incident reduction) and SCE (formalizing agent operations).*

## D2 — AI in the Product

**Reproducible evaluation harnesses enable safe prompt compression for production agents.** [A NeurIPS 2026 Workshop paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31972) presents an offline evaluation framework for a production merchant-facing conversational agent that stays reproducible despite operating on live, mutable enterprise data. The harness serves as the fitness function for GEPA-based prompt optimization under a correctness gate that prevents reward hacking. Result: 3.5–7.6% prompt surface reduction without correctness regression across three model families, with one family showing measurable improvement. The key insight is methodological: you cannot safely compress production prompts without a reproducible evaluation substrate, and that substrate must handle data drift. *Cross-cuts D4 — every percentage of prompt compression directly reduces inference cost.*

**The AI productivity honeymoon is definitively over.** [Atlassian's State of Developer Experience 2026](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/atlassian-devex-2026/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering) reports that developers claiming 10+ hours/week saved by AI plummeted from 68% to 26% year-over-year, while only 28% report noticeably less friction. Most damning: engineering leaders report spending more time managing and correcting AI output than developers themselves. This isn't a failure of AI — it's the predictable consequence of deploying AI without the D4 discipline to manage downstream cost. The 2× output is real, but the ½× downstream cost isn't materializing, which means the speed is borrowed. *Cross-cuts D1 and D4.*

## D3 — Build for Agents

**MCP's trust model is structurally broken for multi-server deployments.** [MCPHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27819) is the first controlled benchmark isolating credential propagation across MCP trust boundaries. Across 3,615 traces from 5 models spanning 147 tasks, policy-violating propagation rates of 11.5–41.3% persist across all models — this is structural, not adversarial. Browser-mediated data flows are the primary vector. Prompt mitigation can reduce rates to 0.3% (98% relative reduction) while preserving 80.5% utility, but effectiveness varies by model, motivating data-flow-aware defenses beyond prompting. Anyone building multi-server MCP architectures needs to treat credential propagation as a first-class design constraint.

**Decode-time provenance binding offers a concrete MCP security primitive.** [Researchers demonstrate cross-modal trust laundering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31974) — instructions concealed via steganography in email-signature images that bypass text scanners and enter multi-agent MCP workflows when decoded by a tool. Their defense combines HMAC-authenticated trust labels with an MCP-maintained provenance ledger and context-taint tracking. The undefended pipeline shows 100% attack success; keyword/regex defenses permit 62.5%; provenance binding blocks all attacks at 0% success. An audited trusted-promotion mechanism restores legitimate-task completion from 67% to 100%. This is the kind of infrastructure-level security primitive that MCP will need to standardize.

**Scale does not purchase fault tolerance in MCP tool-calling agents.** [MCP-Injector](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32555), a protocol-aware fault-injection middleware calibrated to the observed failure distribution of 57,240 issues across 1,774 MCP repositories, reveals that realistic tool faults cause task-completion scores to collapse 60% (from 8.08 to 3.23/10). The 120B model retains no larger share of its fault-free score than the 20B model (p=0.96). Agent framework choice matters more than model scale for fault absorption — a finding that should redirect investment from bigger models toward better error-handling infrastructure. *Also relevant to D4 — unhandled tool faults are a direct source of incidents and support escalation.*

## D4 — Cost of Ownership

**The Atlassian DevEx data is a flashing red warning on downstream cost.** [The collapse from 68% to 26%](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/atlassian-devex-2026/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering) in developers reporting double-digit weekly AI time savings, combined with engineering leaders spending more time correcting AI output, directly threatens the 2× output / ½× downstream cost equation. If AI-generated code requires more management overhead than it saves, the downstream cost of the running system increases — through more incidents from insufficiently-reviewed code, more tech debt from AI-generated solutions that don't match architectural intent, and more support burden from features that don't quite work right. The 28% reporting reduced friction means 72% experience the same or more friction — meaning the agentic factory is generating velocity without reducing the cost of ownership that velocity implies.

**LLM-optimized code is faster but energy-heavier — a hidden runtime cost.** [An empirical study across 11 models and 1,000 EffiBench problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32891) finds that 42 of 44 LLM configurations produce code consuming more energy per problem than human-written reference solutions, with overheads from +4% to +886%. Break-even points require 10⁶–10⁷ executions. The inference energy itself spans 0.79–8.68 MJ per configuration (250–2,800 GPT-4.1 query equivalents). Mixture-of-Experts architectures consume substantially less inference energy than equivalently-sized dense models without sacrificing correctness — making model architecture selection a meaningful sustainability lever. For CTOs: "faster" is not "cheaper to run."

**HarnessMM demonstrates that decision reuse can dramatically cut agentic inference cost.** [Guarded decision reuse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32813) replaces redundant reasoning callbacks with stored executable decisions, achieving 29.5% token reduction on HumanEval (maintaining 90.8% success) and 87.9% token reduction on DeepSeek Harness (while improving success from 71.4% to 85.7%). The mechanism — Executable Decision Graphs with causal dependencies and public guards — operates at the model-tool boundary without modifying model weights. This is a concrete, deployable approach to reducing the per-task cost of the agentic factory while maintaining or improving quality.

## Software Civil Engineering Lens

Today's batch provides strong evidence for the SCE thesis from multiple angles, with an unusually clear signal.

**The AGENTS.md census is the most significant SCE development.** The finding that [26.3% of active repositories now contain agent instruction files](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31715) — rising to 59.6% of the most-starred — represents the spontaneous emergence of a specification artifact in the absence of any standard or mandate. This is analogous to early construction practices developing local building codes before formal engineering standards existed. But the 74% no-rationale rate reveals the critical gap: these are specifications without the reasoning that makes them verifiable, composable, or teachable. The SCE framework predicts this exact phase — practitioners feel the need for blueprints but lack the professional norms to make them rigorous.

**PaperFormalizer is the clearest "human on the loop" implementation yet.** [The pipeline's design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31810) — agent formalizes, human adjudicates ambiguity, toolkit grows across papers — maps precisely to the Specify → Plan → Verify → Apply → Observe lifecycle. The author retains judgment over what a statement means and how gaps are fixed, while the agent handles the mechanical formalization. This is bounded autonomy working in practice: the agent operates within Lean's formal constraints, the human controls the semantic boundary.

**The authorization/recovery paper advances the "codes and norms" pillar.** [Decomposing recovery into four distinct obligations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31916) (checkpoint, dispatch permission, effect evidence, reconciliation permission) and testing them through formal conformance studies is precisely the kind of engineering discipline that the SCE framework calls for. The fact that simpler retry strategies fail 6–12 of 42 histories demonstrates why informal approaches to agent reliability are insufficient.

**The Atlassian data is the strongest counter-evidence to "craft scales."** If 72% of developers experience no reduction in friction from AI, the craft approach — where individual developer skill determines AI effectiveness — is failing at organizational scale. This is the forcing function the SCE thesis predicts: you cannot get the 10× transition from "human in the loop" to "human on the loop" without professionalizing the practice. The gap between what AI can produce and what organizations can reliably deploy is a specification and verification gap, not a capability gap.
