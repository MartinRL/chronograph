## D1 — Agentic Engineering

**Model-harness interaction is the dominant variable in agent performance, not model quality.** A [66-configuration study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00917) across four harnesses (OpenHands, DeepSeek Harness, PI, openJiuwen) and five models on three benchmarks reveals that model rankings *reverse* depending on the harness. Claude leads GPT by 7.94 points in OpenHands but trails by 30.16 in PI. A model's vendor harness is not reliably its best pairing. The practical implication: teams that select a model in isolation and then bolt on an orchestration layer are making a decision with less information than teams who evaluate the pair together. The study releases 6,204 scored trajectories for replication.

**Frontier agents fail the majority of realistic incident-response tasks.** [Incident-Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00648) introduces 20 tasks grounded in real deployed OSS, each running in an ephemeral Kubernetes cluster with injected faults and sustained load. Best-performing frontier models score below 64.3%, consuming an average of 2.81M tokens and 41 turns per trial. Failures span diagnosis, localization, incomplete repairs, and unsafe regressions. This is the most realistic agentic SRE benchmark to date — and it shows that agents cannot yet be trusted for autonomous incident response. (Cross-cutting: D4 — the token cost per incident trial is enormous relative to the value delivered.)

**Auditable formal verification harnesses improve both correctness and cost.** [FORALL-LEAN-AGENT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00885), accepted at NeurIPS 2026 VeriCodeGen, wraps any coding agent with isolated workspaces, axiom audits, and independent proof checking. On VeriSoftBench, it raises GPT-5.6 Sol's success from 93 to 100 out of 100 while *reducing* cost from \$69 to \$62. The key insight: structured harness design that enforces verification evidence can simultaneously improve quality and reduce spend — the opposite of the usual quality-cost tradeoff.

**Praxa makes authority-to-effect transitions explicit and testable in agent execution.** The [Praxa harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00015) separates proposal, authority, dispatch, verified effect, and promotion into distinct deterministic states. While the paper honestly reports that its reliability layer doesn't yet demonstrate superiority (using 37–51% more tokens), its coordination proxy achieved 37% fewer tokens and 34% lower cost at equal accuracy. The contribution is architectural: making each state transition auditable is a prerequisite for governed agent execution in production.

**Stateful policy enforcement keeps agents safe without killing utility.** [Sapien](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00797) introduces a policy engine that specifies permitted tool-call sequences as regular expressions with stateful predicates and deferred policy generation. It blocks 93–95% of attacks on AgentDojo and 62–85% on Toolathlon while staying within a few percent of unconstrained agent utility. This is a practical advance: tool allowlists are coarse; Sapien's sequence-aware policies are context-sensitive, doubling attack prevention on long-horizon tasks versus static lists. (Cross-cutting: D3 — this is how you govern agents consuming your APIs.)

## D2 — AI in the Product

**92.6% of enterprise agent runs that pass output checks contain hidden process-level failures.** [Continuous process-level evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01833) applied to enterprise business-value-determination skills across 240 trials found that of 175 runs passing all final numerical checks, 162 contained evaluator-detected deviations in tool selection, argument validity, or execution order. This has direct implications for anyone shipping AI agents in a product: your end-to-end tests may be green while the agent's reasoning path is brittle, drifting, or producing correct answers for wrong reasons. Dependency attribution reduced mean failed checks from 6.34 to 2.65 root causes per run, suggesting a tractable path to better diagnostics.

## D3 — Build for Agents

**Docker's Sandbox Kit Spec packages agent permissions as OCI images, heading to CNCF.** [Docker announced](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/docker-sandbox-ai-agent/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering) it is bringing a specification that makes what an AI agent may access as portable as the agent itself. By encoding permissions in OCI-format images, this creates a standardized, infrastructure-level mechanism for B2A (Business-to-Agent) capability advertisement. If adopted at CNCF, every Kubernetes-native agent deployment gets a declarative, versionable permission boundary — the container equivalent of capability-based security for MCP/A2A interop.

**Fault-tolerant budget conservation formalizes resource control for multi-agent delegation.** [This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00349) proves that distributed agent networks need exclusive escrow-based resource credits to prevent overspend under real failure modes (lost replies, timeouts, message replays, partitions, DAG join aliasing). Budgets are quantized into dispatch permits bound to lineage, epoch, and idempotency key. TLA+ model checking and crash-injected SQLite experiments validate the mechanism. For anyone building A2A delegation graphs: this is the formal foundation for preventing one runaway child agent from exhausting the parent's budget — a problem that will bite every production multi-agent system.

**The global coherence problem proves multi-agent coordination needs shared state, not smarter models.** [An Observation-Aliasing Impossibility Theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02036) shows that when k indistinguishable worlds require disjoint actions, no amount of reasoning, messages, or sampling can exceed 1/k success — the missing distinction is in the environment, not the model. On TeamBench, ordinary multi-agent teams exceed shared budgets 5/5 times; commit enforcement via a harness-owned shared state drops violations to 0/5. The takeaway for D3: agent-to-agent protocols must include shared-state primitives, not just message passing.

## D4 — Cost of Ownership

**Agentic SRE is currently a net cost amplifier, not a cost reducer.** [Incident-Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00648) shows that frontier agents consume an average of 2.81M tokens per incident trial while achieving below 64.3% success, with failures including unsafe regressions that would *create* new incidents. Until agentic SRE can reliably resolve incidents without introducing regressions, deploying it risks increasing downstream support costs (escalation to 2nd/3rd line, additional incident investigation) rather than reducing them. The 2× output / ½× cost equation currently runs backwards for this use case.

**Process-level behavioral drift in enterprise agents silently erodes the ½× downstream cost promise.** The [enterprise evaluation study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01833) finding that 92.6% of output-passing runs contain trajectory deviations means that agents producing "correct" outputs today may be accumulating hidden technical debt in their reasoning paths. When tool APIs or specifications change — as they inevitably do in enterprise environments — brittle trajectories break unpredictably, creating support incidents that are harder to diagnose because the output *used to be* correct. Process-level monitoring is a prerequisite for sustainable agentic deployment.

**Harness selection can cut inference cost by 75% at equal or better performance.** The [Finding the Right Fit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00917) study shows that GPT scores higher under PI than under DSH at less than a quarter of the cost per task on Terminal-Bench 4. This is a pure factory-cost optimization: same model, different harness, dramatically different token economics. Teams paying for agentic inference should be auditing harness efficiency as aggressively as they audit model selection.

## Software Civil Engineering Lens

Today's batch is a remarkable convergence on the SCE thesis from multiple independent research groups. Five themes stand out:

**1. The "blueprint" gap is being named and addressed.** The [Cybernetic and Epistemic vocabulary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00961) articulates what SCE calls "formal spec" in philosophical terms: every consequential agent choice must carry "the condition under which it would have gone otherwise, in a form a third party can test." Their ORRCF deliberation-recording convention is essentially a lightweight event model for agent decisions. Meanwhile, [Praxa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00015) makes authority-to-effect transitions explicit states — directly analogous to the Specify → Plan → Verify → Apply → Observe lifecycle.

**2. Simulation before deployment is gaining formal grounding.** The [fault-tolerant budget conservation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00349) work uses TLA+ model checking to verify resource safety properties *before* deployment — this is terraform-plan-for-domain-logic applied to multi-agent economics. The [claim-specific verification audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01348) goes further by replacing aggregate scores with evidence verdicts (supported, unsupported, unresolved, not evaluated), each bounded to the conditions under which they hold. This is the agent equivalent of structural load testing.

**3. The "codes and norms" pillar is materializing.** [Sapien's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00797) stateful policy engine defines permitted tool-call sequences as formal regular expressions — essentially building codes for agent behavior. Docker's [Sandbox Kit Spec](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/docker-sandbox-ai-agent/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=AI%2C+ML+%26+Data+Engineering) heading to CNCF is the infrastructure-level codification of what agents may access, analogous to zoning regulations.

**4. The readability → explainability shift challenges but ultimately supports SCE.** Huntley's [argument](https://ghuntley.com/readable/) that software doesn't need to be *readable* anymore, just *explainable*, seems to undermine the craft tradition but actually accelerates professionalization. If humans aren't reading the code, formal specifications (blueprints) become *more* important, not less — you need something to verify against. The [Global Coherence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02036) impossibility theorem reinforces this: you can't substitute intelligence for missing specification of shared state.

**5. The "material datasheets" gap is the most visible absence.** The [model-harness interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00917) study shows that model performance is not an intrinsic property — it depends on the harness, the task, and the interaction between them. We have no standard way to characterize a model's "load-bearing capacity" in a given configuration. This is exactly the datasheet problem: civil engineers know the tensile strength of steel in a specific grade and configuration. We don't yet know the equivalent for GPT-under-PI vs. Claude-under-OpenHands.

Today's needle movement: significant. We are seeing independent researchers converge on the same structural insight — that agent governance requires formal, verifiable, auditable constraints — from at least five different angles. The field is building the vocabulary, the tooling, and the formal foundations for software's professionalization, even if it doesn't always call it that.
