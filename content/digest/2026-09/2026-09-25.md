## D1 — Agentic Engineering

**Recursive AI-led model development reaches frontier quality.** [iCoder-27B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29626) is the most compelling "human on the loop" demonstration to date: human experts encode objectives, stage scaffolds, and permission boundaries as reusable research skills, while agents select experiments, diagnose outcomes, and revise training strategy across SFT, self-distillation, and RL phases. The resulting 27B model outperforms GPT-5.5 and ties Claude-Opus-4.8 on industrial coding benchmarks for RTL design and GPU kernel optimization. The key D1 pattern: humans operate at the specification plane (what to optimize, what constraints to respect), agents operate at the execution plane (experiment selection, data evolution, strategy revision). This is the spec-driven development loop applied to AI R&D itself.

**BaseCamp separates the judgment layer from the execution layer in scientific pipelines.** [BaseCamp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28557) decomposes DNA sequencing into six specialized agents that don't perform sequence analysis — established tools do that — but instead select tools, configure them, interpret outputs, and decide what follows. The explicit "filtering ledger" renders inspectable what filtering otherwise removes without trace, and cross-stage anomaly detection surfaces conditions that execution monitoring misses. This is a transferable architecture pattern (D1/D4): confine LLM reasoning to the judgment layer where it's reliable, preserve deterministic tool guarantees, and make every decision auditable. The local execution model (no data leaves the environment) also addresses a real constraint for regulated domains.

**Governance automation gets a rigorous task-substitution framework.** ["The Last Human Gate"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29345) treats each governance gate as an executable contract and derives conditions for when automation actually reduces total human work — accounting for exceptions, verification, correction, and maintenance. The key finding is sobering: automating most cases can still *increase* labor if the residual-work threshold isn't met. On DGF-Bench (300 synthetic projects), Gemini 3.8 Flash achieves 94.98% strict gate success but only 76.92% complete-route success. A deterministic control passes all 1,700 gates, proving that the gap is in LLM execution of a supplied decision kernel, not in the decision logic itself. This is directly actionable for any team automating review gates. (Cross-cutting: D1/D4)

**SWE-Prometheus benchmarks what no existing coding-agent benchmark measures: engineering governance.** [This benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29465) evaluates six governance dimensions — Tests & CI, Quality Gates, Documentation, Reproducible Environment, Dependency & Security — through paired evidence, clean-environment probes, and behavior gates. Across 10 models on 22 repos, mean Normalized Governance Improvement ranges from 0.057 to 0.576, with behavior-breakage rates up to 23%. A repository-blind template scores 0.272 NGI but only in the easy dimensions; it improves zero repos on Reproducible Environment or Dependency & Security. This draws a hard line between "adding governance artifacts" and "producing execution-backed improvements" — a distinction every engineering leader should demand of their agent tooling. (Cross-cutting: D1/D4/SCE)

## D2 — AI in the Product

**PUBG Ally ships a voice-enabled AI teammate to millions of players across 141 countries.** [PUBG Ally](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29837) is a production-grade embodied agent that combines agentic tool use with real-time game control under strict latency constraints. The architecture splits into a language-model agent (context management, speech interpretation, high-level decisions) and a fast control layer (movement, combat, recovery). Training used data from ~39K live sessions with real players. The production engineering is the real story here: model compression, context compaction, targeted safety training, runtime guardrails, and memory redaction were all required for deployment. Among players whose gameplay was confirmed in records, positive recommendations exceeded negative by 25.1 percentage points. This is D2 at scale — an agent embedded in a product where latency, safety, and player experience are existential constraints, not academic concerns.

## D3 — Build for Agents

**Skilder enforces hard governance boundaries via role-scoped capability delivery over MCP.** [Progressive Skill Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28693) addresses the fundamental problem of exposing enterprise toolsets to LLM agents: oversized context windows, degraded tool selection, and governance violations. Skilder packages capabilities into roles (bundles of skills, tools, instructions, and limits) served through a single MCP server. Because tools reach the agent only inside learned skills, the server enforces scope deterministically — not probabilistically via prompts. Across 13 tasks on six models, no unauthorized tool call or parameter violation executed when the discovery protocol was followed. This is the B2A access control pattern the ecosystem has been missing: structural enforcement that doesn't rely on the model's willingness to comply. (Cross-cutting: D3/D4)

**Semantic commit predicates are the only viable runtime guard for agents mutating infrastructure.** [Stale Does Not Mean Unsafe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29522) runs 3,456 agent trajectories through a deterministic simulator with five non-atomic failure mechanisms. The results are stark: freshness-based guards (the industry default) needlessly block 92–95% of benign concurrent mutations, forfeiting up to 43% of safe task completions. Semantic commit predicates block zero benign operations while eliminating all unsafe commits. Model-side signals are useless — verbal confidence is miscalibrated (ECE ≈ 0.37), and cautionary prompts leave the unsafe rate essentially unchanged. This has immediate implications for any team building agent-to-infrastructure interfaces: you need contracts, not heuristics.

**Safe Skill Retirement formalizes when agent capabilities can be safely pruned.** [This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29543) introduces a two-gate retirement certificate: a candidate reduction must preserve authorized utility while producing zero unauthorized protected effects. Across 2,592 evaluation cells, task-certified reductions remove 94%+ of skill clauses and preserve authorized completion but produce unauthorized protected effects in every bundle. The takeaway for D3: agents operating in physical or privacy-sensitive domains need formal retirement protocols for capability changes, not just benchmark regression testing.

## D4 — Cost of Ownership

**Harness-level routing recovers \$3.3–5.0M/year for a 10K-seat enterprise AI coding deployment.** This directly improves the 2×/½× equation by cutting the agentic factory's largest variable cost without touching output quality. [Control the Harness, Control the Cost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28919) demonstrates that enterprises using default-configured AI coding harnesses (Claude Code, Codex) inherit suboptimal model routing decisions and their bill. The Jev classifier routes prompts at session boundaries, side lanes, and subagent launches — the only points where prompt-cache rebuild costs don't negate savings. The counterintuitive finding: on long tool-heavy sessions, the highest-priced model costs less than mid-tier because of cache economics. The paper maps risks across twenty harnesses and proposes a control plane enterprises can deploy now. This is the most immediately actionable cost-of-ownership paper of the week.

**MeshHeal cuts token cost 56% while improving accuracy for multi-agent systems under gray failure.** This tilts the 2×/½× equation favorably on both sides: better incident resilience (downstream) at lower inference cost (factory). [MeshHeal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29015) addresses the specific failure mode where an agent remains responsive but its task-solving quality persistently degrades — the agentic equivalent of a gray failure in distributed systems. Its two-timescale approach (fast: escalate uncertain outputs to committee review; slow: detect persistent degradation and exclude/reintegrate agents) achieves 0.839 degraded-phase accuracy using 51K tokens per task versus the best baseline's 0.807 at 115K tokens. The peer-review architecture is fully decentralized, making it viable for mesh agent topologies.

**Agent self-assessment is miscalibrated by 29–38 percentage points, creating hidden support and rework costs.** [SpecHarness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29921) quantifies the understanding-execution gap: across seven models, only 79.6–86.4% of extracted task directions are satisfied, while completion-claim rates exceed evaluator pass rates by 28.7–37.9 points. Every false completion claim that reaches production becomes a downstream incident, support ticket, or silent data corruption. The fix is architectural: separate agent proposals from authoritative state, compile specifications into source-linked obligations, and govern finalization through versioned obligation state.

## Software Civil Engineering Lens

Today's batch is the strongest single-day evidence for the SCE thesis I've seen. Five papers independently converge on the same principle: **specifications must be the authority, not the agent.**

**SpecHarness** explicitly separates the "understanding-execution gap" and the "state-authority gap" — the former is a materials problem (model capability), the latter is a governance problem (who signs off). Civil engineering solved this decades ago: the contractor doesn't sign off on structural integrity; the engineer of record does, against codified standards. SpecHarness operationalizes exactly this boundary for software agents.

**The semantic commit predicate paper** provides the most rigorous evidence yet that **contracts beat heuristics** for runtime safety. Freshness-based guards are the software equivalent of "eyeball it" — they reject nearly everything to be safe, destroying productivity. Semantic predicates are the equivalent of load calculations: precise, formal, and correct. The finding that deleting a single declared clause converts exactly its fault family into unsafe commits is a perfect demonstration of why formal specs aren't optional — they're load-bearing.

**SWE-Prometheus** is the first benchmark that treats engineering governance as a measurable output rather than a compliance checkbox. The distinction between "adding governance artifacts" and "producing execution-backed improvements" maps directly to the SCE thesis: just as a civil engineer's blueprints must be backed by structural analysis, not just documentation, agent-produced governance improvements must be backed by execution evidence.

**The Last Human Gate** contributes the residual-work threshold — a formal criterion for when automation reduces total human effort versus merely displacing it. This is the cost accounting that the profession has been missing. The fact that a deterministic control passes all gates while the best LLM achieves only 77% complete-route success precisely locates the gap: it's in execution reliability, not decision quality. This maps to the SCE "simulation before construction" pillar — the decision kernel works; the agent's execution of it is the weak link.

**iCoder-27B** demonstrates the "human on the loop" pattern at its most ambitious: humans provide the specification (objectives, boundaries, operating procedures), agents run the execution loop (experiment selection, diagnosis, strategy revision). This is the 10% → 10× transition in practice: minimal human involvement at the specification layer producing frontier-quality output at the execution layer.

The collective message: the professionalization of software engineering through formal specification isn't a philosophical aspiration — it's becoming an engineering necessity. Agents without contracts are contractors without blueprints. Today's papers quantify what happens when you try to build that way: 23% behavior breakage, 38-point completion miscalibration, 95% false rejection rates. The profession is being forced toward formalization not by regulation but by the practical impossibility of scaling agents without it.
